Why this matters at work

Anjali handles client escalations for a Dubai office. She types "write reply to angry customer" and gets a bland three-paragraph letter with no order number and no promise in it. She deletes it and writes the email herself, the way she always has. Six short parts, in one paragraph. The first draft comes back usable instead of generic.

Workplace example

Why "write an email" fails

The tool is not guessing badly. It is guessing — because you left it nothing else to do.

Try it — copy-paste prompt
Copy this prompt
Write a reply email to a client whose delivery slipped. Order 4471 was promised on 12 March and will now arrive on 15 March, delayed by a transport strike. Tone: apologetic but calm. Under 120 words. Give me a subject line too.
