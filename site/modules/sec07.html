What Never to Paste Into AI

The five red lines, the two-second test, and exactly what to type instead when you are in a hurry.

Why this matters

One paste, and it is out of our hands

Farhan in accounts needs a payment-reminder letter. He pastes the customer ageing report — 340 names, phone numbers and outstanding amounts — into a free AI site. The letter that comes back is excellent. The report is now on a server we do not control.

We cannot retrieve it, cannot delete it, and cannot tell an auditor where it went.
What changes

You will know in two seconds whether something is safe to paste — and exactly what to type instead.

The five red lines

What never goes in, and why

Five things that never go in

1
Customer or employee personal data
Names with phone numbers, addresses, ID numbers, salaries. This is the one regulators count.
2
Anything lifted from a signed contract
Prices, terms, penalty clauses. The client agreed to share those with us, not with a third party.
3
Financial records and pricing
Margins, cost sheets, bank details, unpublished results. A competitor would pay for these.
4
Login details of any kind
Passwords, API keys, one-time codes. A prompt box is not a password manager.
5
Anything marked confidential or internal
Board papers, legal advice, unannounced plans. If it carries a marking, the marking is your answer.

Why one paste is hard to undo

People expect a paste to behave like a draft email they can unsend. It does not.

1

You paste

The text leaves your laptop and travels to the tool's servers.

2

It is stored

Most services keep the conversation. Free accounts usually keep it longest.

3

It may be reused

On consumer accounts, chats can be used to improve the service unless you switch that off.

4

You cannot recall it

No delete button reaches every copy. This is the part people do not expect.

What goes wrong

The part people do not expect

What actually goes wrong

A reportable incident

Under India's DPDP Act and UAE data protection law, personal data leaving our control is an incident we may be required to report.

A client relationship

Clients ask how their data is handled. "An employee pasted it into a chatbot" is not an answer that survives a renewal meeting.

It can resurface

Text used for training can, rarely, appear in another user's answer. You have no way to check whether yours did.

The test that takes two seconds

Could this text identify a person or a customer?
Yes

Do not paste it

Take out the names, numbers and addresses first, or describe the situation in your own words instead. The tool does not need to know who it is to write well.

No

Safe to paste

Dates, quantities, reasons, general descriptions. Everything it needs to write with, and nothing that could harm anyone if it leaked tomorrow.

Strip it down

Turn a file into safe facts

Turn a file into safe facts

What Farhan pasted
  • The full ageing report: 340 rows with names, phone numbers, credit limits and outstanding amounts.
  • Plus the covering note naming the two largest defaulters.
  • Every one of those names is now outside the company, permanently.
What he should have typed
  • "Write a firm payment-reminder letter for an invoice 45 days overdue."
  • "Second reminder. Long-standing client, we want to keep the relationship."
  • Nothing identifying anyone — and the letter that comes back is just as good.

The tool never needed the names. It only ever needed the situation.

Do this now: strip a real request

  1. Open something you were genuinely about to paste this week.
  2. Delete every name, phone number, address and ID number.
  3. Replace exact amounts with a range, and exact dates with "45 days overdue".
  4. Paste what is left. Add the real details yourself afterwards, in Outlook.
Copy this prompt
Write a firm but polite payment reminder for an invoice that is 45 days overdue. This is the second reminder. The client is long-standing and we want to keep the relationship. Under 130 words. Include one clear sentence asking them to confirm a payment date.
No name, no invoice number, no amount. It still writes the whole letter.

Say it in your own words instead

Describing a situation takes about fifteen seconds and removes the risk entirely.

Copy this prompt
I need to reply to a customer complaint. A delivery arrived three days late and two cartons were damaged. We accept the delay. We want to offer a replacement for the damaged cartons but not a discount. Draft a 120-word reply, apologetic but not grovelling.
Everything the tool needs. Nothing it should not have.
What comes back
  • A complete reply covering the delay, the damage and the replacement offer.
  • No customer name, no invoice number and no address went in — because none of it was needed.
  • You add the name yourself, in Outlook, where it belongs.

If you have already pasted

The first hour is worth more than the next month. Here is the whole procedure.

  1. Stop. Do not delete the conversation — it may be needed as a record.
  2. Write down what you pasted, into which tool, and roughly when.

Reporting early is not the problem

Nearly every incident that became serious became serious because somebody waited.

Four habits that keep you clear

What is fine to paste

This module is not asking you to stop using AI. Most of what you need help with is perfectly safe.

Your own draft

Something you wrote yourself, names removed. Ask it to shorten it, or fix the English.

Public information

A published circular, a public spec sheet, anything already sitting on the open internet.

A situation, described

"A delivery was three days late and two cartons were damaged." No names, and no meaning lost.

The one-line test

Would I be comfortable if this exact text landed in a competitor's inbox tomorrow?

If the answer is anything other than a clear yes, it does not go in.

  • A clear yes — paste it and carry on.
  • Not sure — strip the names, then ask yourself again.
  • No — describe the situation in your own words instead.

Recap

Never-paste rules on one screen

Five things never go in

Personal data, contract terms, financials, logins, anything marked confidential.

Facts, not files

Describe the situation. Do not paste the document the situation came from.

Names come out first

Names, numbers and addresses before anything else. Those are what regulators count.

Approval is more than an account

The specific tool, account and use all need approval — not just a work login.

You cannot recall a paste

No delete button reaches every copy. Prevention is the only control you have.

Report it the same day

An hour later there are options. A month later there is only an apology.

Would I be happy for this exact text to arrive in a competitor's inbox tomorrow? If not, it does not go in.

Glossary

Words used in this module

Personal data
Any information that points to one identifiable person — a name, a phone number, an ID, sometimes an address on its own.
PII
Personally Identifiable Information. Another term for personal data, used mostly in US and UAE documents.
DPDP Act
India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act. It sets out how personal data must be handled and when a breach must be reported.
Data breach
Personal data going somewhere it should not. It does not need a hacker — one paste is enough.
Approved tool
The tool, account and use the company has actually approved — not just any work login.
Prompt
Everything you type into an AI tool. It leaves your device and is usually stored.
