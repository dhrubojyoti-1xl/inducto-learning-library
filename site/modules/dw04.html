Data Analysis with AI

Getting a straight answer out of your numbers, without letting it do the arithmetic.

Why this matters

Deepa's total is out by nine lakh

Deepa pastes forty rows of dispatch values into an AI tool and asks for the total and the top five customers. The answer is instant, formatted beautifully, and the total is wrong by about nine lakh rupees.

A number that reached the MIS pack before anyone re-added it.
What changes

Let it describe and structure. Let Excel do the arithmetic.

What you'll be able to do

By the end of this module

  • 1Split a task into the arithmetic part and the language part
  • 2Never let a model add up a column again
  • 3Ask questions of data that AI can genuinely answer
  • 4Turn a set of figures into a summary that states no causes
  • 5Analyse company numbers without exposing customers
Why totals go wrong

It predicts, it does not calculate

It predicts, it does not add

A total is text to it, produced the same way as a sentence. Short sums usually work. Long ones drift.

1

You paste forty rows

It reads them as text, not as a column.

2

It produces a total

The most likely number-shaped answer.

3

It looks exactly right

Correct digit count, sensible magnitude.

4

Nobody re-adds it

Because it came back formatted and confident.

Where the arithmetic breaks

Usually fine

Small and simple

  • Adding four or five numbers
  • A percentage of one figure
  • Comparing two values
  • Converting one unit to another
Do not trust

Long or layered

  • Totalling a column of forty rows
  • Averages across grouped categories
  • Percentage change across many periods
  • Anything that feeds a report or an invoice
The safe split

Excel calculates, AI explains

Excel calculates, AI explains

The two-minute workflow

  1. Do the sums in Excel. SUM, AVERAGE, a pivot if you need one.
  2. Copy out only the handful of results that matter.
  3. Paste those results into the tool, not the raw rows.
  4. Ask for the summary, with no causes and no extra figures.
Copy this prompt
Below are calculated figures from our monthly pack. Write a 150-word summary naming the three largest movements and nothing else. Do not calculate anything. Do not suggest causes. Do not add any figure that is not in my list.
"Do not calculate anything" is the line that keeps this safe.
Questions it can answer

About shape, not about truth

Questions it answers well

Anything about the shape of the data, once you have supplied the numbers.

How do I say this?

"Describe a fall from 1.4 to 2.1 per cent without sounding alarmist."

What should I check?

"What would you want to see before accepting this trend as real?"

What shape is this?

"Turn these eight results into a table with three columns and no commentary."

The questions-to-check prompt

Copy this challenge prompt
Here are the figures and the conclusion I have drawn from them. List the five questions a sceptical manager would ask before accepting this conclusion. Do not answer them and do not comment on whether my conclusion is right.
It cannot verify your data. It is very good at predicting the questions.
What comes back
  • Five questions, usually including one about sample size or period.
  • No opinion on your conclusion, because you ruled that out.
  • You go and answer two of them before the meeting, not during it.
Describing without explaining

Movements, never causes

Movements, never causes

Let it explain
  • "Rejections rose to 2.1 per cent, likely due to operator changeover during the shift pattern change."
  • Your data contains no shift information at all.
  • Maintenance investigate changeover for two days.
Describe only
  • "Rejections rose from 1.4 to 2.1 per cent, the largest movement in the pack."
  • Every word traces to a figure you calculated.
  • You then ring the line supervisor and find the real cause.

Describing is arithmetic you already did. Explaining is a claim about the world, and it was not there.

What must never be pasted

A data extract usually carries customers, not just numbers.

Aggregate first. Paste the summary, never the raw table.

  • Customer or account names against values
  • Employee names against output or attendance
  • Anything with a phone number, address or ID column
  • Unpublished financial results or margins
Do this now

Summarise a real set of figures

Do this now: summarise figures

  1. Open the last set of numbers you had to write about.
  2. In Excel, produce the six results that actually mattered.
  3. Replace any customer names with "Customer A, B, C".
  4. Paste those six lines with the prompt on the right.
Copy this prompt
Here are six calculated results. Write a 120-word summary for a manager. Name the three biggest movements in order of size. Do not calculate, do not suggest causes, do not add any figure I have not given you. Plain sentences, no bullet points.
Six lines in, a usable paragraph out, nothing identifying anyone.

Four checks before it goes out

Five mistakes people really make

1
Pasting the raw table and asking for a total
The number comes back formatted and wrong, and it looks exactly like a real total.
2
Letting it name a cause
It will offer one confidently. Somebody then spends two days on the wrong line.
3
Pasting customer names with values
That is a customer list with commercial data attached, leaving the company.
4
Trusting a percentage it produced
Percentages are calculations. If you did not work it out, do not print it.
5
Asking it to "analyse this data"
Vague in, vague out. You get an essay about the general shape of numbers.

When to use a chart tool instead

Do I need a number, or a sentence about a number?
A sentence

Use AI

Describing a movement, drafting the commentary, shaping a table, listing what a sceptic would ask. All language work.

A number

Use Excel

Totals, averages, percentages, rankings, variances. Anything that will be printed, invoiced or reported as a figure.

What good analysis looks like

The spreadsheet

Every figure calculated, checkable and yours.

The six results

Aggregated, anonymised, pasted in.

The paragraph

Written by the tool, describing only those six.

Each layer is smaller and safer than the one before it. By the time anything reaches the tool, there is nothing left to get wrong or to leak.

The data rule

If it will be printed as a number, a spreadsheet must have produced it.

AI writes the sentence around the number. It never produces the number.

  • Arithmetic — Excel.
  • Wording — the tool.
  • Causes — you, having checked.

Recap

Data analysis on one screen

It does not calculate

A total is produced as text. Over a long column it drifts, and looks fine.

Excel first, always

Totals, averages, percentages and rankings before anything is pasted.

Paste results, not raw rows

Six aggregated lines instead of forty rows with customers attached.

Describe, never explain

It can word a movement. It cannot know why the movement happened.

Anonymise before pasting

Customer A to E works perfectly well and exposes nobody.

Check every figure back

Each number in the text must match your spreadsheet exactly.

If it will be printed as a number, a spreadsheet must have produced it.

Glossary

Words used in this module

Aggregate
A calculated summary — a total, an average, a top five — rather than the underlying rows.
Anonymise
Replacing names and identifiers with labels like Customer A, so nobody can be identified.
Movement
A change between two periods. Something you calculate and the tool describes.
Cause
Why something changed. Only established by you, never by the tool.
Prompt
Everything you type in: your calculated figures and the constraints.
Output
What comes back. Your draft, which you are responsible for checking.
