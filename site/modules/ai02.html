Generative AI

What it actually makes, why every answer is different, and where it beats a search box.

Why this matters

Shalini sends a line she never wrote

Shalini runs marketing for a Pune plant. She asks an AI tool for a team note about a moved meeting. The note is excellent. It also mentions a quarterly bonus review that nobody has scheduled. Two people email HR about it.

An afternoon spent explaining a sentence she did not write.

You will know why it does that, and how to catch it in four seconds.

What generative means

It builds, it does not fetch

It builds. It does not fetch.

There is no file it is copying from. Every sentence is assembled the moment you ask for it.

1

You describe it

In plain sentences, with your own facts in them.

2

It builds

Each word chosen to fit the words before it.

3

Nothing is copied

There is no stored answer being handed back to you.

4

You get something new

Text that did not exist a second earlier.

A generator is not a database

A search index

Stores pages. Returns the ones that match.

A generative model

Stores patterns. Builds a fresh answer every time.

What that means for you

Never quote it. Always read it.

This is why two colleagues asking the same question get different wording. Neither answer was retrieved. Both were made.

Terms on this page: Generative AI

What it can make

Text, structure, shorter versions, options

Four things it makes well

It makes more than text

Images and slides

Fine for a rough internal draft. Risky for anything a customer will actually see.

Formulas and code

Excel formulas, small scripts. Always test one row before you trust the column.

Translation

Hindi to English, English to Arabic. Good for the gist, never for a contract.

Why answers change

The same question, two answers

Same words, different answer

A search box

Same words, same result

  • Everyone gets the same ten links
  • Run it tomorrow and get the same list
  • You can cite the page you found
  • The result is a place, not a sentence
A generator

Same words, new answer

  • The wording differs every single time
  • Two colleagues get two versions
  • There is no page you could cite
  • The result is text, made only for you

Turn that into an advantage

Variation is a problem when you did not ask for it, and a gift when you did.

Copy this prompt
Give me three versions of this message: one formal, one friendly, one very short. Message: our delivery to the Sharjah site will now arrive on Thursday instead of Tuesday, because of a customs hold.
Three versions cost you the same as one.
Why this works
  • You pick a tone instead of arguing with the tool.
  • Seeing three side by side makes the choice obvious.
  • The facts stay identical across all three.
Search box or generator?

Pick the right one first time

Which one do I need?

Will anyone ask me where this came from?
Yes

Use search

A duty rate, a rule, a specification, a news report. If you will have to show the source, you need the actual page.

No

Use the generator

A reply, a summary, a first draft, a set of options. You already hold the facts and you need them turned into sentences.

Gaps get filled, never flagged

A missing detail does not produce a blank. It produces something plausible.

The tool has no way to leave a hole in a sentence, so it closes it.

  • Names of people, products and places you never supplied
  • Figures and dates that look exactly right
  • Quotes and references that exist nowhere
  • Policies it assumes a company like ours must have

Terms on this page: Hallucination

Do this now

Make something in two minutes

Do this now: make something

  1. Open your approved assistant.
  2. Think of one message you owe somebody today.
  3. Paste the prompt on the right and press Enter.
  4. Read it once, change one detail, and send it.
Copy this prompt
Write a short internal note telling the team that the Friday review has moved from 11 am to 3 pm, and that the monthly numbers are still due on Thursday evening. Under 70 words. Friendly but clear.
Works as written. Swap in your own meeting afterwards.

The four seconds that matter

Sent unread
  • The note reads beautifully and mentions a "quarterly bonus review" nobody has scheduled.
  • Two people email HR about the bonus that afternoon.
  • You spend an hour explaining a sentence you did not write.
Read once, then sent
  • The same note, read through once before sending.
  • You spot the invented line and delete it in four seconds.
  • Nobody ever knows it was there.

Generated text is a first draft with your name at the bottom. Reading it once is the whole job.

Four habits with generated text

Five mistakes people really make

1
Sending the first version unread
Invented meetings, bonuses and policies reach the team with your name on them.
2
Asking it to "find" something
It does not search. It produces text that resembles a finding.
3
Trusting a precise-looking number
A figure quoted to one decimal place is the most convincing kind of invention.
4
Putting generated images in front of customers
Almost-words and wrong details are obvious, and they read as carelessness.
5
Asking again to check the first answer
You get a second generated answer, not a check on the first one.

Where it genuinely earns its keep

First drafts

A blank page becomes 200 words you can edit. This alone saves hours in a week.

Changing shape

Long into short. A list into a table. Formal into plain. Hindi into English.

Options on demand

Ten ideas in twenty seconds. You are choosing rather than inventing.

The one habit that matters

If you did not supply it and cannot check it, take it out.

That one rule removes almost every problem generated text can cause you.

  • Names you never typed
  • Numbers you cannot trace
  • Rules nobody has confirmed

Recap

Generative AI on one screen

It builds, it does not fetch

Every answer is constructed fresh. There is no page behind it.

Different every time

Two colleagues asking the same thing get two answers. Neither is the record.

Gaps get filled, not flagged

A missing fact produces a plausible invention, never a blank space.

Strong on shape, weak on truth

Drafting, shortening, restructuring, options. Not lookups, not policy, not figures.

Read every line

Anything you did not supply and cannot verify comes out before you send.

Never a source

It is your draft, that you checked. That is the only honest description of it.

Generative means it makes something new every time. That is the power and the whole risk, in one sentence.

Glossary

Words used in this module

Generative AI
A system that builds new text, images or code rather than retrieving something that already exists.
Model
The trained system behind the app. ChatGPT, Copilot and Gemini are apps built around models.
Prompt
Everything you type in: the request plus the facts you choose to supply.
Hallucination
A confident, invented answer. Usually a name, a number, a date or a policy.
Training data
The public text a model learned from, up to a fixed cut-off date.
Output
What comes back. Your draft, which you are responsible for checking.
