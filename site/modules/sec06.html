Safe Use of AI at Work

Which tools are approved, which settings to switch off, and how to keep a trail you can actually explain.

Why this matters

Harish cannot answer a simple question

Harish runs projects in Abu Dhabi. A client asks, during an audit, whether any of their drawings have been processed by AI tools. He genuinely does not know. Nor does anyone else, because nobody kept a record.

A question we could not answer, in front of a client.
What changes

Approved tools, the right settings, and a habit of noting what you used.

Approved and unapproved

More than an account

Approval is more than being logged in

The same model can be safe or unsafe depending on the account you signed in with — but the account alone does not make a use approved. The tool, the configuration and what you are using it for all have to be approved too.

Unapproved

Personal login, free tier

  • No agreement anybody here has read
  • Chats often used to improve the service
  • No record of what was shared
  • Nothing can be deleted on our behalf
Approved

[COMPANY INPUT NEEDED: approved AI tool]

  • Covered by terms the company agreed
  • Business tiers normally exclude training on your data
  • Access is managed and can be withdrawn
  • Somebody can answer questions about it later

The settings that matter

Two switches

Two switches worth finding

Training on your data

Consumer accounts often use chats to improve the service. Business tiers usually do not. Check which you are on.

Chat history

Turning history off usually turns training off too, and leaves less sitting there to leak.

Shared workspaces

Check whether colleagues can see your conversations. Sometimes they can.

Settings are not a substitute

Switching training off reduces the risk. It does not make confidential data safe to paste.

The text still travels, is still stored, and is still outside our control.

  • Settings reduce reuse.
  • They do not stop transmission.
  • The paste rules still apply.

Keeping a trail

Answering the audit question

Answering the audit question

No record
  • "Has any of our material been through an AI tool?"
  • "I do not think so, but I could not say for certain."
  • That answer is worse than a yes with a record behind it.
One line per use
  • "Drafting assistance only, on the approved tool, with no client drawings uploaded."
  • Backed by a note in the project file, written at the time.
  • The question takes thirty seconds and ends there.

Nobody is asking you to justify using a tool. They are asking whether you know what happened to their material.

The one-line note

Copy this note format
AI use: [what for — drafting / summarising / formatting]. Tool: [approved tool name]. Data provided: [description, e.g. dates and quantities only, no client documents]. Output checked by: [your name]. Date: [date].
Five fields, twenty seconds, kept in the project file.
Why this works
  • "Data provided" is the field the auditor cares about.
  • "Checked by" makes clear a person owned the output.
  • Written at the time, it takes seconds. Reconstructed later, it is guesswork.
Do this now

Check your own setup

Do this now: check your setup

  1. Open every AI tool you have used for work in the last month.
  2. Check which account you are signed in with on each.
  3. Find the data and privacy settings and turn training off.
  4. Remove any browser extension you cannot name the vendor of.
Copy this prompt
Explain, for someone with no technical background, what the difference is between a consumer AI account and a business one in terms of how data is handled. Six lines maximum. Do not recommend specific products.
Useful background before you check your own settings.

Saying that AI helped

The three rules that never change

Approved tool, work account

Every time, including for quick questions.

Facts, never files

Describe the situation. Do not upload the document.

A person checks the output

And a person is accountable for it afterwards.

Settings, tools and policies will change. These three have not changed since the first of these tools arrived, and will not.

Five mistakes people really make

1
Using a personal account for a quick question
Quick questions carry the same data as slow ones, with none of the protections.
2
Installing a browser extension that rewrites text
Everything you select goes to a service nobody here has assessed.
3
Letting a note-taking bot into a client call
You have recorded people who never agreed to be recorded.
4
Assuming settings make pasting safe
They reduce reuse. The data still leaves, and is still stored.
5
Keeping no record at all
"I do not know" is the worst possible answer to an audit question.

The safe use rule

Approved tool, work account, facts not files, and a person who checked it.

Four conditions. If all four hold, you are almost always fine.

  • Approved tool and work account.
  • Facts described, files kept back.
  • A named person checked the output.

Glossary

Words used in this module

Approved tool
The tool, account and use the company has actually approved — not just any work login.
Consumer tier
A free or personal subscription. Terms nobody here negotiated, and often training on your input.
Training on your data
Using what you type to improve the service. Usually switchable on business tiers.
Shadow AI
AI tools in use at work that nobody assessed or approved. Usually browser extensions and bots.
Audit trail
A record of what was used, on what, by whom. Written at the time, not reconstructed.
Accountability
A named person owns the output, regardless of what helped produce it.
