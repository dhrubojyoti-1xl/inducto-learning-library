Meeting Notes & Follow-ups

Turning an hour of discussion into decisions and owners, before anyone leaves the room.

Why this matters

Nobody remembers who agreed what

A weekly production meeting in Pune runs for an hour. Everyone leaves clear about the decisions. Nine days later, two of the four actions have not started, and each person believed somebody else owned them.

Nine days lost, and the same discussion happening again.
What changes

Three columns — decision, owner, date — sent within the hour.

What you'll be able to do

By the end of this module

  • 1Turn rough notes into decisions and owners in five minutes
  • 2Write actions nobody can misread as somebody else's job
  • 3Send follow-ups the same day, every time
  • 4Take useful notes without recording anyone unlawfully
  • 5Chase an overdue action without starting an argument
Notes nobody uses

Why minutes fail

Why minutes fail

Most meeting notes record the conversation. Almost nobody needs the conversation.

What gets written

The discussion

  • Who said what, in order
  • Points raised and considered
  • Background nobody disputed
  • "It was agreed to look into it"
What gets used

The decisions

  • What was actually decided
  • One named owner per action
  • A date, not "next week"
  • What is still open, and who will close it

"We agreed to look into it"

Unowned action
  • "The team will review the supplier issue and revert."
  • Four people read it. Each assumes one of the others owns it.
  • Nine days later nothing has started and nobody is at fault.
Owned action
  • "Priya to get written confirmation of the revised lead time. By Thursday 14th."
  • One name, one deliverable, one date.
  • If it slips, everybody knows on Friday morning.

"The team" is not an owner. Every action needs one person's name on it.

Decisions, owners, dates

The only three columns

Only three columns matter

Everything else is optional

Decisions carry

Six months later, this is the only part anyone goes back to read.

Owners prevent drift

An action with two owners has none. An action with a team has fewer.

Dates create movement

"Soon" and "next week" are both read as "whenever".

Rough notes to actions

Five minutes after the meeting

Five minutes after the meeting

Copy this prompt
Below are my rough notes from a meeting. Produce three sections and nothing else. DECISIONS: one line each. ACTIONS: one line each, with the owner's name and a date. OPEN: anything not settled. Use only my notes. Where no owner or date was given, write [OWNER?] or [DATE?].
Type your notes however rough. Order does not matter.
Why this works
  • [OWNER?] shows you exactly what to chase before you send it.
  • Three sections stop it summarising the discussion.
  • Five minutes now saves the nine days later.

The markers are the point

What the markers give you
Send me back only the lines containing [OWNER?] or [DATE?], as questions I can ask in one message. Keep them short and put them in the order they came up in the meeting.
One message to the group closes every gap at once.
What comes back
  • Three or four short questions you can send in a single message.
  • Usually one action everybody assumed somebody else was doing.
  • The notes go out complete, the same afternoon.
Recording and consent

Before you press record

Before you press record

A recording or transcript of a meeting is personal data about everyone in it.

Consent, storage and who can access it all have to be settled first.

  • Tell everyone before recording starts, not afterwards
  • Use only the tool the company has approved for this
  • Never upload a recording to a personal AI account
  • Never record a conversation about a person's performance

Do this now

Process a real meeting

Do this now: process a meeting

  1. Open the notes from your most recent meeting.
  2. Remove any customer names and replace them with roles.
  3. Run the three-section prompt on them.
  4. Send one message asking about every [OWNER?] and [DATE?].
Copy this prompt
Turn my notes into three sections and nothing else. DECISIONS, one line each. ACTIONS, one line each with an owner and a date. OPEN, what is unsettled. Use only my notes. Mark missing owners [OWNER?] and missing dates [DATE?].
The single most useful five minutes after any meeting.

Chasing without an argument

Copy this chaser
Write a short message chasing an action that is three days overdue. Facts: agreed at the review on the 6th, due the 14th, no update received. Tone: neutral and factual, not passive-aggressive, no apology from me. Under 50 words. End by asking for a new date.
Neutral is a real instruction and it works.
Why this works
  • "Not passive-aggressive" removes the usual edge.
  • "No apology from me" stops it softening your position.
  • Asking for a new date gives them something easy to do.

Four habits after every meeting

Five mistakes people really make

1
Writing "the team will"
Four people read it and each assumes one of the others owns it.
2
Recording without telling people
A transcript is personal data about everyone in the room, taken without consent.
3
Minuting the discussion instead of the decisions
Two pages nobody reads, and the one decision buried in the middle.
4
Sending notes three days later
By then people have half-remembered a different version and started acting on it.
5
Using "next week" as a deadline
It is read as "whenever", and it always is.

The follow-up that ends a meeting

One message, same day

Decisions, actions with owners, open items.

No discussion in it

Nobody re-reads who argued what, ever.

Under 150 words

Long enough to be complete, short enough to be read.

The whole value of a meeting is what happens in the nine days afterwards. This message is what decides whether anything does.

The meeting notes rule

Every action needs one name and one date, or it is not an action.

That single test removes most of what goes wrong after meetings.

  • No name — nobody does it.
  • No date — nobody hurries.
  • Sent tomorrow — already too late.

Glossary

Words used in this module

Action
Something one named person will do by a stated date. Anything else is a discussion point.
Owner
The single person accountable. Never a team, never two people.
Open item
Something not settled, with a named person bringing it back.
Transcript
A written record of everything said. Personal data about everyone present.
Personal data
Anything identifying a person, including their recorded voice and words.
Prompt
Everything you type in: your rough notes and the structure you want back.
