Advanced Prompting

Breaking a big job into steps, setting limits the answer must respect, and checking the work as you go.

Why this matters

Zainab asks for everything at once

Zainab handles compliance for an Abu Dhabi group. She asks one AI tool to read a policy, find the gaps, write the fixes and draft the board note. What comes back is confident, tidy and wrong in the middle, where nobody looks.

A board note built on a gap analysis nobody checked.
What changes

Four small asks instead of one big one, checked between each.

What you'll be able to do

Why one big ask fails

The middle nobody checks

The middle nobody checks

A four-stage request produces one polished answer. You can only check the ends of it.

1

Stage one is fine

You gave it the document, so this part is grounded.

2

Stage two drifts

It infers gaps you cannot see it inferring.

3

Stage three builds on it

Fixes for problems that may not exist.

4

Stage four reads well

A confident note resting on stage two.

One ask versus four

One big ask

Everything at once

  • One answer, four stages hidden inside it
  • You can only judge the final wording
  • An early error is invisible by the end
  • Correcting it means starting again
Four small asks

Checked between each

  • Four answers you can each judge in a minute
  • An error is caught where it happened
  • You correct one step, not the whole job
  • The final draft rests on things you approved
Chaining

Four small asks, checked

Four asks instead of one

Terms on this page: Chaining

The tick between the steps

The value is not the four prompts. It is the ten seconds you spend between them.

Read step one

Does this list match the document I pasted? Delete anything that does not.

Approve or cut

Tick the items you accept. The next step uses only those.

Feed forward

"Using only the three I ticked..." Every later step rests on approved ground.

Constraints

Limits it must respect

Limits it must respect

Copy these constraint lines
Constraints: use only the text I have pasted. Do not add clauses, dates or figures that do not appear in it. If something needed is missing, write [MISSING: what it is] and continue. Maximum 200 words. No introduction and no closing summary.
Paste this block under any serious prompt.
Why this works
  • "Only the text I pasted" is the strongest single line here.
  • [MISSING] converts silent invention into a visible flag.
  • The word limit stops it padding a thin answer.

Four constraints worth reusing

Show the working

Where reasoning matters

Ask it to show its working

For anything with reasoning in it, the steps are more checkable than the conclusion.

Copy this prompt
Work through this in visible steps before answering. First list the figures you are using and where each came from in my text. Then show the calculation. Then give the answer. If a figure is not in my text, stop and say so instead of estimating it.
Useful for anything with arithmetic or a judgement chain.
What comes back
  • A short list of figures with their origin marked against each one.
  • The working set out in lines you can follow and check.
  • An answer you can accept or reject for a specific reason.

What visible steps do not fix

Shown working is easier to check. It is not evidence that the working is right.

The steps are generated the same way the answer is.

  • It can show tidy, wrong arithmetic.
  • It can cite a figure it invented.
  • You still have to open the source.
Do this now

Chain a real task

Do this now: chain a task

  1. Take a job you would normally ask for in one message.
  2. Write down the stages you would do it in by hand.
  3. Ask for stage one only, with the constraint block attached.
  4. Read it, delete what is wrong, then ask for stage two.
Copy this prompt
Step 1 of 4. Using only the text I have pasted below, list what it requires us to do. Facts only, one line each, no interpretation and no recommendations. If something is unclear, write [UNCLEAR] beside it rather than guessing.
Say "step 1 of 4" out loud in the prompt. It keeps the answer narrow.

Chained versus all at once

One message
  • "Read this policy, find the gaps, fix them and write the board note."
  • Nine hundred confident words. The gap list inside it was never shown to you.
  • You approve a note whose middle you have not seen.
Four messages
  • Four short answers, each judged in about a minute.
  • You cut two invented requirements at step one.
  • The board note covers three gaps you personally confirmed.

The same total time. The difference is where your attention went.

When chaining is worth it

Would a wrong middle step reach a customer or a board?
Yes

Chain it

Compliance, pricing, proposals, anything signed. Break it up and approve each stage before the next one uses it.

No

One ask is fine

Internal notes, first drafts, rough summaries you will rewrite anyway. Chaining here is ceremony, not control.

Three habits worth keeping

Five mistakes people really make

1
Asking for the whole job in one message
You get one polished answer and can only check its beginning and its end.
2
Chaining without reading between steps
Four prompts with no checks is just a slower version of one prompt.
3
Saying "be concise" instead of a number
Concise means nothing. Two hundred words means two hundred words.
4
Trusting shown working as proof
Tidy steps can lead to a wrong answer, confidently and legibly.
5
Letting step three quote step one loosely
Say "using only the three I ticked" or it will quietly reintroduce the ones you cut.

The advanced prompting rule

Break the job at the points where a mistake would be invisible later.

That is what separates a chain from four prompts in a row.

  • Split where errors hide.
  • Check before you feed forward.
  • Constrain what it may invent.

Recap

Advanced prompting on one screen

Split where errors hide

Break the job at the points a mistake would be invisible in the final answer.

Check between every step

Four prompts with no reading between them is one prompt with extra steps.

Feed forward only what you approved

"Using only the three I ticked" stops cut items reappearing.

Constrain the source

"Use only what I pasted" plus [MISSING] for anything absent.

Numbers, not adjectives

"200 words" works. "Be concise" does not.

Shown working is checkable, not correct

It makes an error findable. It does not make the answer right.

Break the job where a mistake would otherwise be invisible, and check it there.

Glossary

Words used in this module

Chaining
Breaking one large request into ordered steps, checking each before the next uses it.
Constraint
A limit the answer must respect: a source, a word count, a forbidden habit.
Step-by-step
Asking the tool to show its intermediate reasoning rather than only the conclusion.
Prompt
Everything you type in: context plus instructions plus your facts.
Hallucination
A confident, invented answer. Chaining makes these easier to catch early.
Output
What comes back. Your draft, which you are responsible for checking.
