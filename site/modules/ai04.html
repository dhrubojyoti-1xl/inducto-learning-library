AI Limitations

Where these tools break, why they break exactly there, and what to reach for instead.

Why this matters

Rajesh quotes a price that never existed

Rajesh handles purchase for an Ahmedabad unit. He asks an AI tool for the current price of a steel grade. It answers with a figure per tonne. He builds a quotation around it. The real rate is 19 per cent higher.

A quotation the company has to honour, or withdraw and explain.

You will know which questions it cannot answer, before you ask one.

The five hard limits

What it structurally cannot do

Five things it cannot do

1
It cannot look anything up
Unless the tool visibly shows a link it visited, no page was opened and no system was queried.
2
It cannot know today's anything
Rates, stock, prices, news. Its knowledge stops at a fixed date it will not always tell you.
3
It cannot see our systems
No ERP, no CRM, no shared drive. Our order numbers and clients simply do not exist for it.
4
It cannot tell you it is unsure
Every answer arrives in the same confident tone, whether solid or entirely invented.
5
It cannot carry consequences
It will not be in the meeting when the number is wrong. You will.

Why the gap gets filled

It is built to produce the most likely next words. A gap is just a place where something likely goes.

1

You ask something

The question implies a fact should exist.

2

It has no fact

Nothing in its patterns matches your specific case.

3

It builds a likely one

A number, a name or a date shaped like a real one.

4

It reads as certain

Nothing in the wording marks it as a guess.

The cut-off date

Why last month is missing

Everything recent is missing

Public text, up to a fixed date

Everything the model learned from. It stops there.

After that date

Circulars, rate changes, news, new products.

Your company, at any date

Never included, at all, ever.

Ask about a change from last month and it will not say "I do not know". It will describe a change that sounds about right.

No

How a process generally works, what a term means, how to phrase something. Stable knowledge, and easy for you to sanity-check.

Numbers and arithmetic

Where confidence is highest

Numbers are the weakest point

A figure looks like evidence. That is exactly why an invented one does the most damage.

Never trust

Numbers it produced

  • Prices, rates, duties, penalties
  • Percentages quoted to a decimal place
  • Dates of rules, launches or changes
  • Any total it calculated from a long list
Safe to use

Numbers you supplied

  • Figures you pasted in from a system
  • Dates you typed yourself
  • Totals you worked out in Excel
  • Anything you can point at a source for

Make it show its working

Copy this checking prompt
For every number in your answer, tell me in brackets whether it came from the text I gave you or from your own general knowledge. If it came from your own knowledge, say so plainly rather than presenting it as a fact.
This does not make it accurate. It makes the guess visible.
Why this works
  • Invented figures get labelled instead of blending in.
  • You see immediately which lines need a real source.
  • It costs one sentence and saves a wrong quotation.
What to use instead

The right tool per limit

The right tool per limit

Two minutes that save a quotation

  1. Write down the one number the whole document depends on.
  2. Ask yourself where that number actually lives.
  3. Go and get it from there. It is usually two clicks.
  4. Then hand the tool the number and ask for the wording.
Copy this prompt
Write a one-page quotation covering letter. The rate is 62,400 per tonne, valid for 15 days, ex works. Payment terms 30 days. Tone: confident and brief. Under 180 words. Do not add any figures I have not given you.
Every number in it came from you. That is the whole point.
Designing around it

Ask so it cannot hurt you

Ask so it cannot hurt you

Question that invites invention
  • "What is the notice period under UAE labour law?"
  • It answers with a specific period, stated plainly and without qualification.
  • You have no way to tell whether it is current, or ever was.
Question that cannot invent
  • "Here is the clause from our contract. Explain it in plain English."
  • It explains the words you actually pasted in.
  • Every sentence traces back to something on your screen.

Bring the fact to the tool. Never ask the tool to bring the fact.

Four ways to blunt the risk

Where people get caught

Every one of these produced a confident, wrong answer for somebody last year.

None of them looked risky at the moment of asking.

  • "What is the current GST rate on this category?"
  • "Summarise the new labour circular for me"
  • "What is the standard warranty in our industry?"
  • "Add up these forty line items and give me the total"

What it is still excellent at

Limits are not a reason to avoid it. They are a map of where to point it.

Wording anything

Emails, summaries, explanations, refusals. The facts come from you, the sentences from it.

Restructuring

Long into short, list into table, dense into plain. Nothing to invent, so nothing invented.

Listing what to check

"What should I verify before signing this?" is a question it answers genuinely well.

Five mistakes people really make

1
Believing a number because it is precise
62,417 reads as researched. It was generated the same way as the sentence around it.
2
Asking about "the new" anything
New means after the cut-off. It will still answer, and the answer will be plausible fiction.
3
Taking a legal answer at face value
It has no jurisdiction, no date and no liability. You have all three.
4
Letting it total a long list
It is producing likely text, not calculating. Long arithmetic drifts and looks fine.
5
Assuming a confident tone means a solid answer
Tone is constant by design. It is not a signal of anything at all.

The limitation rule

If the answer matters and you cannot check it, you cannot use it.

Not a rule about AI. A rule about anything you sign your name to.

  • Check it — then use it.
  • Cannot check it — go to the source.
  • No time to check — no time to send.

Recap

AI Limitations on one screen

It cannot look anything up

No pages, no systems, no live data, unless the tool visibly shows you otherwise.

Recent means invented

Anything after the cut-off gets a plausible answer rather than an honest blank.

Numbers are the weak point

A precise figure reads as researched. It was produced like every other word.

Confidence is not a signal

The tone never changes, whether the answer is solid or entirely made up.

Bring the fact to the tool

Paste the clause, the figure, the circular. Then ask for wording.

If you cannot check it, do not use it

That is a rule about anything you sign, not a rule about AI.

It never says "I do not know". That single missing behaviour is the source of almost every AI problem at work.

Glossary

Words used in this module

Cut-off date
The point where a model's training text stops. Nothing after it was ever seen.
Training data
The public text a model learned from, up to that cut-off date.
Hallucination
A confident, invented answer. Usually a name, a number, a date or a policy.
Grounding
Giving the tool the actual source text so its answer has something real to stand on.
Prompt
Everything you type in: the request plus the facts you supply.
Output
What comes back. Your draft, which you are responsible for checking.
