Teamwork & Collaboration

Handing work over so it arrives finished, and asking for help without friction.

Why this matters

Anand's handover takes three days

Anand passes a costing job to another team in Chennai. He sends the file and says "please complete". Three days and eleven messages later, they still do not know which rate card to use or when it is due.

Three days of messages, for a job that took four hours.
What changes

Four lines with any handover, and the questions never start.

What you'll be able to do

By the end of this module

  • 1Hand work over so it comes back finished, first time
  • 2Ask for help in a way that gets a fast answer
  • 3Say what "done" means before anyone starts
  • 4Raise a problem with a colleague without a confrontation
  • 5Give feedback that changes something, not just feelings
The four-line handover

What arrives finished

Four lines, no questions

Most handover friction is not disagreement. It is four missing lines that nobody thought to write.

The same job, two ways

"Please complete"
  • A file, and two words.
  • Eleven messages over three days about scope and rates.
  • It comes back on Thursday in the wrong format.
Four lines
  • "Costing for the Sharjah tender, using the 2026 rate card."
  • "Done means every line priced, with exclusions listed at the bottom."
  • "By Wednesday 3pm. If a rate is missing, ask Priya rather than estimating."

Four lines take ninety seconds to write and save three days of messages. This is the highest-return habit in the module.

Defining done

The commonest failure

"Done" is the failure point

Two people can both be right about whether something is finished, and disagree completely.

You meant

Ready to send

  • Priced, checked and formatted
  • Exclusions listed explicitly
  • In the client template
  • Ready to go out under your name
They heard

Numbers filled in

  • All the cells have figures in them
  • Assumptions in their head, not written
  • In whatever format they work in
  • Expecting you to finish it off

Say what done looks like

Copy this wording
Done means: every line priced using the 2026 rate card, exclusions listed at the bottom, in the client template, and ready to send without me editing it. If anything is missing, leave it blank and note it rather than estimating.
"Ready to send without me editing it" is the line that does the work.
Why this works
  • It describes the finished state, not the activity.
  • It names the standard, so nobody has to guess.
  • "Leave it blank and note it" prevents invented figures.
Asking for help

Fast answers

Getting a fast answer

1
"Can I pick your brain sometime?"
No question, no time, no scope. It gets a yes and then nothing happens.
2
"Are you free?"
Nobody answers yes to this without knowing what it commits them to.
3
A paragraph of background before the question
The reader has to work out what is being asked before deciding whether they can help.
4
Asking without saying what you tried
They repeat the two things you already did, and you both lose ten minutes.

The three-line ask

Copy this structure
I am stuck on the Sharjah costing. I have tried the 2026 rate card and the old tender file, and neither has a rate for site supervision. Do you know where that lives, or who would? Two minutes would do it.
Problem, what you tried, specific question, time required.
Why it works
  • "What I tried" stops them suggesting what you already did.
  • A specific question can be answered in one line, often immediately.
  • "Two minutes" tells them the size before they commit.
Raising a problem

Without a confrontation

Without a confrontation

Most cross-team problems are process problems that look like people problems.

Copy this wording
"The costings have come back after the deadline three times this quarter, and it is squeezing the review. I do not think that is anyone dragging their feet — I suspect we are handing them over too late. Could we look at the dates together?"
Name the pattern, not the person. Offer to look together.
Why this works
  • "Three times this quarter" is a fact, not a complaint.
  • Explicitly removing blame lets them engage without defending.
  • "Look at the dates together" makes it a shared problem.

Where to raise it

Is this the first or the fourth time?
First

Direct and informal

A call or a quiet word with the person involved. Almost every first occurrence is a misunderstanding, and it resolves in five minutes without anyone else knowing.

Fourth

Involve the managers

With the dates written down and no commentary about anybody. A repeated pattern is a process problem, and process problems need the people who own the process.

Do this now

Rewrite one handover

Do this now: rewrite one handover

  1. Find something you handed over that came back wrong.
  2. Write the four lines you did not write at the time.
  3. Notice which of the four would have prevented it.
  4. Use all four on the next thing you hand over.
Copy this prompt
Turn my rough note into a handover with exactly four labelled lines: WHAT, DONE MEANS, BY WHEN, IF STUCK. Use only what I have written. Where I have not said something, write [NEEDS ANSWER] rather than inventing it.
The [NEEDS ANSWER] markers are the questions you would have been asked anyway.

Feedback that changes something

Five mistakes people really make

1
Handing over a file with "please complete"
Three days of messages establishing what you meant by complete.
2
Assuming done means the same to both of you
You meant ready to send. They heard numbers filled in. Both were reasonable.
3
Asking to "pick someone's brain"
No question and no time attached. It gets agreed to and never happens.
4
Raising a repeated problem as a personal one
The fourth late delivery is a process fault, and naming a person ends the conversation.
5
Giving feedback three weeks later
Nobody remembers the specifics, so it lands as a general judgement about them.

Working across sites

Say the time zone

"3pm Dubai time" removes an hour and a half of ambiguity with an Indian office.

Write, then call

Written first gives everyone the same words. The call resolves what the writing could not.

Name one contact

"Ask Priya" beats "ask the costing team", which reliably means asking nobody.

The collaboration rule

Say what done looks like, when it is due, and who to ask when it is not clear.

Almost every cross-team problem is one of those three lines missing.

  • Done means what, exactly.
  • By when, with a time.
  • Ask whom, by name.

"Ready to send without me editing it" removes the whole ambiguity.

Give a time, not a day

"Wednesday 3pm" starts work on Tuesday. "Wednesday" starts it on Wednesday.

Name one person to ask

Glossary

Words used in this module

Handover
Passing work to somebody else. Needs four lines, not a file and two words.
Definition of done
What the finished output looks like, stated before anyone starts.
Escalation
Involving managers. Appropriate for a repeated pattern, not a first occurrence.
Blocker
Something stopping progress. Should reach a named person within hours, not days.
Feedback
One specific thing, its effect, and what you would like instead. Within two days.
Buffer
Extra time added to absorb delays. Useful, and it hides the problem if used instead of fixing it.
